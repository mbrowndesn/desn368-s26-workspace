DESN368 · Spring 2026

Looking Back

Five honest snapshots from a semester of making things for the web.

01 / The Threshold

When Code Clicked

For me it really clicked while I was finishing the Christopher Nolan tribute site. All the stuff that wrecked me on the recipe page, like closing my tags and figuring out the structure, just started feeling normal and I wasn't having to think about it so hard anymore. I could actually picture what I wanted and make it happen instead of guessing. By the time I finished that site I felt like I knew what I was doing, and I realized it really did just come down to practice.

02 / The Pit

The Hardest Part

The recipe page was definitely my pit. It was the first thing I really tried to code and I was so lost at first, struggling so much to learn a whole new language. I kept forgetting to close my tags, I couldn't get my images to link right, and I didn't really get when I was supposed to use a div versus a header, or what id's and classes were even for. Honestly there wasn't one big fix, it just took practice. The more I did it the more it made sense, and by the end I actually understood the structure instead of just guessing at it.

03 / The Pivot

Finding a Direction

My design honestly changed a lot from where I started. I didn't really have a locked plan at the beginning, so I figured out my direction as I went. At first I was leaning toward a clean, bright look, but the more I pulled references the more I realized that wasn't what I actually wanted. I kept coming back to risograph prints and punk poster collages, that grainy printed feel with photo cutouts and spiky stars and loud overlapping color. So I pivoted into a "color outside the lines" style, with fluorescent pink and orange, grain over everything, and black and white cutouts. It ended up way more me than where I started, and honestly the back and forth of figuring it out taught me more about my own taste than if I'd just stuck to a first idea.

04 / The Process

What I'd Do Differently

If I changed one thing, it would be how I managed my time and my expectations. I had a habit of starting assignments late, and I'd also build this big vision in my head of exactly how something should look, then feel let down when I couldn't pull it off, especially early on when I was still learning. Looking back, a lot of that came from not giving myself enough time to actually get there. If I did it again I'd start sooner and let my ideas grow as I built instead of expecting the finished version right away.

05 / The Future

Where This Goes

Going forward I think having both design and code is going to be a big deal for me. I'm studying visual communication design and I've always loved creative media like design and film and editing, so being able to actually build the things I picture, not just mock them up, feels like a real advantage. I understand now how my designs actually come together on the web, which makes me a better designer to work with even if someone else does the building. I want to keep getting better at both, because knowing how design and code work together is going to set me apart.
